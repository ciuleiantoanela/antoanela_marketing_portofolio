How Romanian shoppers
discover fashion.

An independent consumer behaviour study exploring how Romanian women discover new clothing products — online, in-store, and everything in between. Informed by firsthand retail observations during my time in fashion retail at Mohito, Bucharest.

Researcher Antoanela Ciulei

01 — Methodology

How the study was conducted.

This study draws on qualitative observations and informal customer interviews conducted during my role as Brand Ambassador and Sales Promoter at Mohito, a women's fashion brand under the LPP Romania portfolio. Over 8 months of daily client-facing interactions, I systematically noted how customers described finding the store, discovering products, and making purchase decisions.

Observations were enriched with a structured survey of 80 respondents (women aged 25–65, Bucharest) conducted independently in Q3 2025, using a self-administered online questionnaire distributed via social media and personal network. The sample skews toward Mohito's core demographic: working women, 30–55.

Researcher's Note

Mohito's target customer is a mature, working woman — typically 30 to 55 years old. This shapes the findings significantly: discovery behaviour in this segment differs considerably from younger fast-fashion consumers, and those differences are a central theme of this study.

02 — Key Findings

Where discovery happens.

The most consistent pattern across both field observation and survey data was that Mohito's core customer — women aged 30 and above — overwhelmingly discovers new products through the brand's own website, not through social media. This stands in sharp contrast to broader fast-fashion trends dominated by TikTok and Instagram.

03 — Customer Segments

Who walks through the door.

Field observation revealed three distinct shopper profiles with meaningfully different discovery behaviours, decision-making styles, and service needs. Understanding these segments is critical for aligning both digital and in-store strategy.

04 — Purchase Barriers

Why they hesitate.

Even when customers arrived having already discovered a product, conversion was not guaranteed. Three consistent barriers emerged across all age segments — with one particularly striking pattern unique to Mohito's mature customer base.

💸
Price sensitivity
Customers frequently cited price as a reason to pause — particularly for non-essential items. Even when the product was clearly liked, the cost created friction.
~62% of hesitations
⏳
"I need to think about it"
A common response — often a proxy for uncertainty about fit, occasion, or value. Customers who left to "think" rarely returned, regardless of intent.
~48% of hesitations
🌸
"Do I deserve this?"
A pattern unique to mature women: guilt around self-indulgence. Customers would say they weren't sure they "needed" it or whether it was okay to treat themselves.
~35% (ages 40+)
👗
Fit & occasion uncertainty
Customers unsure how or where they'd wear the item — especially for non-basics. Styling guidance from staff significantly reduced this barrier.
~29% of hesitations
Key Insight

The "do I deserve this?" barrier is rarely discussed in retail literature but was consistently present among women aged 40 and above. It suggests an opportunity for brands like Mohito to frame purchases not as indulgences but as intentional self-investment — in communications, visual merchandising, and staff training.

05 — Key Takeaways

What the data tells us.

01
The website is the primary discovery channel for mature Romanian shoppers
72% of the 30–55 segment first encountered a product on the brand website. TikTok and Instagram play a minimal role for this demographic — investment in UX, editorial content, and website product photography matters far more than social media frequency.
02
In-store experience is disproportionately important for the highest-value segment
The 45–65 age group — Mohito's most loyal and highest lifetime-value customers — are primarily in-store discoverers. Their retention depends almost entirely on the quality of human interaction, not digital marketing.
03
Emotional barriers, not price, are the hardest to overcome
While price was cited most frequently, the "do I deserve this?" barrier was the hardest to shift. Customers who were reassured — through personalised attention and genuine engagement — converted at a significantly higher rate than those who were given discounts.
04
Young shoppers are noisier but less valuable to this brand
The 18–29 segment arrived via TikTok, browsed extensively, and converted at a lower rate. Their expectations around price and brand identity were often misaligned with Mohito's positioning — suggesting the brand's social strategy should focus on retention of its core segment rather than acquisition of a younger one.
06 — Recommendations

What brands should do next.

🌐
Invest in website editorial content
Since the website is the dominant discovery channel, product pages should include styling guides, occasion suggestions, and size-inclusive photography targeting the 30–55 segment.
🤝
Train staff on emotional conversion
Equip sales teams to recognise and gently reframe self-doubt in mature shoppers. Language like "you deserve to feel good in what you wear" is more effective than a promotional discount.
💌
Build loyalty programmes for in-store visitors
The highest-value customers are in-store discoverers. A loyalty programme that rewards repeat visits — not just purchases — would significantly increase retention in the 45–65 segment.
📸
Reframe social content for the core audience
Rather than chasing younger audiences via TikTok trends, focus Instagram content on aspirational but relatable mature women — real styling, real occasions, real lives.
🛍️
Reduce "I need to think about it" drop-off
Introduce wishlist features on the website and in-store QR codes that let customers save items — reducing the friction of leaving without buying and creating a re-engagement opportunity.
📊
Track discovery source at point of sale
A simple "how did you hear about this?" question at checkout — online or in-store — would give brands real-time discovery data at scale, making future decisions less anecdotal.
